## Quick Answer
Adaptive Difficulty is an Experimental toggle added in the Sep. 5 update. It defaults to OFF and can be enabled or disabled at any time in the Game Settings. When enabled, the system adjusts enemy damage, health, and break resistance up after sustained player success and down after repeated deaths. For the official announcement, see the Sep. 5 update: [/mortal-shell-ii/updates/september-5-update/].

## How to Turn Adaptive Difficulty On or Off
1. Pause the game and open the Game menu.
2. Go to Game > Game Settings.
3. Find "Adaptive Difficulty" and toggle it ON or OFF.

Common mistakes
- Don't assume toggling requires a new run or restart; the option is in Game Settings and can be changed directly.
- If you can't find it, confirm your game has the Sep. 5 update installed. See the Updates hub for recent patches: [/mortal-shell-ii/updates/].

## How Adaptive Difficulty Works
Official (confirmed) behavior:
- The option is marked Experimental in the Sep. 5 announcement.
- It is optional and defaults to OFF.
- Sustained winning increases enemy damage, health, and break resistance.
- Sustained deaths reduce those values and add a limit on the maximum share of player health a single attack can remove.
- Difficulty shifts are gradual and will move back toward normal balance over time.
- The announcement does not provide exact multipliers, numeric caps, or a complete interaction table with every mode or build.

Community-reported notes (not official):
- Players are discussing interactions with Night Mode and boss damage; some report changes in boss feel when Adaptive Difficulty is active. Treat these as player observations, not confirmed mechanics.

## Does It Go Above Normal Difficulty?
Yes. The official notes state sustained success can push enemies beyond the game's original highest standard difficulty. The announcement does not confirm infinite scaling or give numeric caps, so while enemies can become tougher than the previous maximum, the exact ceiling is not published.

## Does Adaptive Difficulty Make the Game Easier?
It can. Sustained deaths reduce enemy damage, health, and break resistance and introduce a cap on how much of your health a single attack can take, which can make encounters more forgiving. However, because the system is bidirectional, continued success will push challenge back up.

## Adaptive Difficulty and Night Mode
- Night Mode is a separate difficulty option. According to the official announcement, Night Mode does not become easier when Adaptive Difficulty scales downward.
- If you want Night Mode's fixed, intended challenge, don't rely on Adaptive Difficulty to soften Night Mode automatically.

## Break Resistance and Parries
Officially, Adaptive Difficulty increases enemy break resistance when it scales upward. Community reports note that higher break resistance can change the feel of parry- or break-focused builds, making break/timing windows feel different. These are player observations and are not provided as official multipliers.

## Should You Enable It?
Editorial guidance (inference):
- Good candidates for enabling Adaptive Difficulty:
  1. Players who want a dynamic, changing challenge that responds to performance.
  2. Experienced players and NG+ runs seeking added variability.
  3. Players who want a Night Mode–level test but with additional oscillating challenge (note that Night Mode itself won't be softened by downward scaling).
- Not ideal for enabling:
  1. Players who need a fixed, repeatable difficulty for testing builds or comparisons.
  2. Speedrunners or anyone requiring consistent enemy stats run-to-run.
  3. Players who need precise, numeric expectations for enemy values (the system's exact numbers are not published).

Label: Editorial — the above are recommendations and inferences, not official mechanics.
