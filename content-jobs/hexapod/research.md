# Hexapod — research notes

Research date: 2026-08-24
Scope: Item 27 only. No map coordinates. These notes are the factual boundary for the article writer; claims that differ by source, run, or patch are explicitly qualified.

## Answer-first fact set

- **Location:** Hexapod is the final boss of **Withered Shoals**, the southwest Corrupted Gate in the Ruins of Mammon. The dungeon is entered through the **Gate of Mammon**.
- **Nearest practical Beacon / route anchor:** **Nameless Pass Beacon** is the last named checkpoint repeatedly used for the approach. GamingProMax also names **Ossinite Falls Beacon** earlier in Withered Shoals and **Hexapod’s Burrow Beacon** as the post-boss Beacon. The stable player-facing route is: enter Withered Shoals through the Gate of Mammon, progress through the dungeon, activate Nameless Pass, follow the nearby path, go below Ruk through the bone tunnel, optionally collect the Depleted Gland, then jump to the void to trigger the arena cutscene and fight.
- **Post-fight checkpoint:** Sources say a Beacon appears after the fight; GamingProMax calls it Hexapod’s Burrow Beacon and notes that mist can make it hard to spot. It is reasonable to tell the player to activate/cleanse it before leaving, but do not invent an exact interaction sequence not supported by the sources.
- **Prerequisites:** The player must be able to reach the Mammon region and enter the Withered Shoals Corrupted Gate. No checked source proves a fixed universal boss order or an exact number of earlier bosses required before Hexapod. Do not present “Droeg first” as a hard requirement; treat any suggested order as route context only.
- **Optional companion:** A green summon sign before the void can unlock/summon **Proxima** for 1 Glimpse. Sources associate her with Hexapod and report that she draws aggro and can use the Triarch Repeater. This is optional assistance, not an entry or victory requirement.

## Route and preparation

1. Reach the Gate of Mammon in the Ruins of Mammon and enter Withered Shoals.
2. Activate the Ossinite Falls Beacon and then the Nameless Pass Beacon if they are still inactive; GamingProMax lists both as Withered Shoals checkpoints.
3. From Nameless Pass, follow the nearby route until the area with Ruk. GamingProMax says to drop below Ruk first and return through the bone tunnel to collect the **Depleted Gland** before jumping to the void. The checked sources do not establish whether the Depleted Gland is required, so call it optional/useful rather than mandatory.
4. Before the void jump, use the summon sign if you want Proxima. A 1-Glimpse cost is reported by GamingProMax and the local Proxima page; it is not a prerequisite.
5. Jump to the void. A short introduction plays and the encounter starts immediately afterward.

Preparation advice should remain evidence-bounded: heal and replenish at the Beacon, keep the arena center readable, reserve stamina for lateral dodges, and bring a weapon/Shell the player already controls. Do not invent a required level, damage type, or item.

## Fight mechanics, phases, tells, and counters

### Shared mechanic

- The creature repeatedly turns to face the player. GamingProMax therefore recommends attacking from the front during recovery windows rather than chasing its sides or back. PatchCrazy instead describes moving around the boss while its shell is closed. Preserve this difference as positioning advice, not a universal law.
- The most reliable general loop is: watch the tentacles/shell, wait for the attack or opening to resolve, land a short two- or three-hit punish, then reset. Do not keep attacking when the shell opens or when a red warning appears.
- Both checked fight guides describe highly visible telegraphs. GamingProMax says the tentacles spread or position before melee attacks; PatchCrazy emphasizes the shell opening as the signal to stop attacking and dodge.

### Phase descriptions and source conflict

- GamingProMax presents a two-phase structure: Phase 1 is tentacle melee; Phase 2 adds the underground burrow/eruption after an unblockable red-warning slash.
- PatchCrazy presents three stages and says the first two are similar shell/melee stages, while the third adds a ranged attack and an underwater/dive attack. It also says critical-hit staggers can advance the stages.
- Because the sources disagree on phase count and use different labels, write “early melee phase(s)” and “later phase” or attribute the two-phase/three-stage descriptions. Do not claim an exact HP threshold or exact phase count as settled fact.

### Attack tells and counters

| Attack / behavior | Source-supported tell | Counter / punish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lateral tentacle slash | Tentacles sweep horizontally across the body; the motion is slow and wide. | Dodge backward for room, or dodge through with iframes if skilled; verify the sweep direction. Re-engage only after the swing. |
| Triple tentacle stab | Three tentacles line up and drive forward in a straight line. | Dodge sideways out of the line; sources describe a short follow-up opportunity beside the body. |
| Overhead slash | Outer tentacles lift and come down in a slow, clear arc. | GamingProMax calls this the easiest attack to parry; otherwise dodge backward. A short recovery window supports a quick two-hit punish. |
| Tentacle flurry / running slash | Multiple tentacles slash rapidly while the creature moves toward or away from the player. | Create distance and let the sequence end; do not try to roll through every hit individually. |
| Squid Surge charge | Hexapod jumps backward, a red indicator flashes, then it charges in a straight line. | Dodge sideways, not backward, so the charge passes. The failed charge leaves a reported one-to-two-second recovery; this is a high-confidence punish window. |
| Phase-transition red-warning slash | A red warning accompanies a slash that GamingProMax describes as unblockable/unparryable and used to create space. | Back off and give it room; do not force a parry. Treat this as the lead-in to the underground sequence. |
| Underground burrow / eruption | In the later phase, Hexapod digs underground; a red indicator tracks at/on the arena floor or toward the player. | Dodge sideways as the indicator approaches. The eruption should miss and briefly expose the boss for a few hits. Keep the arena center available. |
| Later ranged attack | PatchCrazy reports a ranged attack in its third stage; the page does not give a settled attack name or projectile pattern. | Do not parry; move laterally and dodge continuously until it ends. Do not create excessive distance because the source says that can provoke a rapid underwater/dive attack. |
| Underwater/dive attack | PatchCrazy says the boss may dive during the later stage and re-emerge rapidly from another direction. | Unlock the camera, face away, run while dodging, then relock after re-emergence. This counter is single-source and should be labelled accordingly. |
| Front grab after stagger | GamingProMax reports an unblockable grab directly in front as the boss recovers from a stagger. | Reposition toward a flank before recovery if possible; do not stand directly in front during the wake-up. This is a guide-reported caution, not independently confirmed. |

## Rewards

- **Hexapod Core:** a Tarstone/ability that works with the **Axatana**. GamingProMax and Nerdschalk report a **60 Resolve** cost and a rapid succession of slashing/striking attacks. PatchCrazy confirms the Hexapod Core drop but does not provide the Axatana/Resolve details. The Axatana compatibility and 60 Resolve figure should be attributed to the checked weapon/boss sources and treated as patch-sensitive.
- **Gloom:** checked sources conflict. PatchCrazy and Nerdschalk list **38,753 Gloom**; GamingProMax lists **54,250 Gloom**. The safe article claim is that Hexapod grants a large Gloom payout, while preserving the conflict instead of selecting one number as universal. If a number is shown, label it source-reported and version/run-sensitive.
- **Ovum/Ova:** GamingProMax says the post-fight Beacon grants an Ovum after cleansing; GameSpot explains that the last Beacon in a major dungeon appears after the main boss and contains Ova for campaign progression. Use “cleanse the post-fight Beacon and collect the Ova/Ovum” as the immediate next step, with the naming variation preserved if needed.
- **Achievement/trophy:** “Something in the deep…” is listed by XboxAchievements, TrueAchievements, Gamer Guides, and PatchCrazy as awarded for defeating Hexapod. No checked source identifies a special pre-kill action or a missable condition. State that the normal defeat unlock is documented and that no Hexapod-specific missable was found in this pass; do not promise that every platform/bug state is identical.

## Shell / weapon evidence

- **Proxima:** the associated optional spirit summon. GamingProMax reports that she pulls aggro and uses the Triarch Repeater, creating room to apply a status effect or punish. Treat this as the strongest source-backed companion recommendation, not a mandatory build.
- **Tiel:** PatchCrazy recommends Tiel because perfect dodge grants temporary invisibility; GamingProMax also says Tiel’s Shadow Strike pairs well with Squid Surge punishes. This is a recommendation, not proof that Tiel is best.
- **Sester Genessa / Black Needle:** PatchCrazy suggests a Genessa + Black Needle setup, with duplicates, Moonshine, and ranged-stagger/critical upgrades. These are build suggestions from one guide and should not be phrased as prerequisites.
- **Forgotten Crossbow:** Gamer Guides recommends the Forgotten Crossbow with Shuddering Stone for knockback when Hexapod lunges. GamingProMax also mentions a sidearm/crossbow as optional ranged chip and notes that some players use it for knockback. Do not claim a guaranteed interruption unless clearly attributed.
- **Status effects:** GamingProMax suggests applying Stasis, Burn, or Lightning while Proxima holds aggro and calls Stasis especially useful because it slows the boss. This is practical guide advice; no checked source proves an exclusive weakness.

## Trophy / missable status

- “Something in the deep…” — **Defeat Hexapod**. Cross-checked with XboxAchievements, TrueAchievements, Gamer Guides, and PatchCrazy.
- No checked source says the achievement requires a particular Shell, weapon, attack, summon, or pre-kill interaction. No Hexapod-specific missable condition was found. Keep this separate from other bosses’ missable tasks.

## What happens next

1. After the kill, locate the post-fight Hexapod’s Burrow Beacon in the misty arena area and cleanse/activate it.
2. Collect the Ova/Ovum associated with the cleared dungeon Beacon.
3. Return to Mammon exploration or fast travel from the newly activated Beacon. The six major Corrupted Gate encounters are described in the broader site sources as route-flexible; do not prescribe Isaac or another boss as mandatory next content unless the source supports the player’s exact save state.
4. Use the Hexapod Core with the Axatana if you own that weapon; see the Axatana and Tarstones guides for the broader upgrade context.

## Practical insights

- The red-icon Squid Surge is not a cue to retreat straight backward; lateral movement is the repeatedly recommended answer because backward movement can leave the player in the charge lane.
- The first attempt is most likely to fail at the later burrow/eruption or dive, not at the slow tentacle telegraphs. Keep the middle of the arena open and watch the floor indicator instead of running randomly.
- Proxima’s 1-Glimpse summon can buy time to apply a Tarstone or ranged setup, but solo victory remains supported by the guides and no summon is required.
- Use short punish strings. The safest damage comes after the charge or the burrow eruption; chasing the turning creature around its sides is less reliable than waiting for recovery.

- **Gloom payout:** 38,753 and 54,250 are both reported. Treat as source-specific, not guaranteed.
